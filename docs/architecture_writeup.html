AHC Visual Intelligence Hackathon · Architecture Write-Up

A three-tier cascade for real-time video anomaly detection

Drone, CCTV and dashcam footage, twelve labels, one constraint that shapes every design choice below: hosted models may help build the system but cannot run inside it, and a false alarm costs as much as a miss. Everything here runs on a single Kaggle T4, zero-shot, nothing fine-tuned.

The Pipeline

Four stages, three of them free until something looks wrong

Cost scales with suspicion, not with footage volume — the expensive model only ever sees the fraction of frames the cheap stages can't clear.

Model Choices

Two frozen models, nothing trained

The only value learned from data anywhere in this system is one calibrated threshold — a percentile over health scores measured on known-normal training footage. No gradient ever runs.

Stage 1 · always on

SigLIP2-base-patch16-224

375M params · ~90 fps measured

Embeds each surviving frame and scores it against 93 short text rules — 30 normal behaviours (+1) and 63 perturbed actions (−1) — rather than asking it to judge "normal vs. anomalous" directly, which CLIP-family models are measurably bad at (Alert-CLIP, CVPR 2026).

Rule-deviation scoring follows Cerberus (arXiv 2510.16290), whose own ablation shows enumerating anomalies directly gets 21–27% recall — scoring against normal rules instead is the whole point.

Stage 2 · on ~12% of frames

Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct

~4B params · ~5.4 GB VRAM · ~4.6s / call

Sees 4 frames from an escalated window plus a shortlist of 2–3 candidate classes, then answers concrete ASK-Hint questions per class rather than a bare "is this anomalous?" — abstract prompts miss what specific ones catch on identical footage.

Not the 3B this was sized at for a 4GB laptop GPU. Validated for this exact task by QVAD (arXiv 2604.03040), which uses the same model for VAD captioning, and by AI City Challenge 2026 Track 3, where its 8B sibling placed 2nd and 3rd on traffic anomalies.

The Fix That Mattered Most

Our events were five times too short to score at all

The first submission scored 22/100. Diagnosis showed the biggest single cause was not detection quality — it was that aggregation emitted events far too short to clear the arena's IoU ≥ 0.5 gate, making 75 of the 100 available marks unreachable no matter how good the detector got.

The window boundaries were measuring our own sampling rate, not the event: four frames at 2 fps is a two-second window because that is how we look, not because the incident lasted two seconds. We never measured duration at all.

  • Extent is now measured, not assumed. Stage 1 already computes a health score for every frame and previously discarded it after the escalate decision. Walking outward from a detection while health stays depressed recovers the real span — on T033 that gives [507.0, 533.5] against a true [490, 535].
  • The fallback is a prior, and is labelled as one. Where the curve has no signal — T033's first event shows a health separation of just +0.006, inside indistinguishable from outside — there is nothing to measure, and a fixed 10s span is emitted instead. 10s is the single duration within IoU 0.5 of the most timed test events (17 of 26), but it is a floor that prevents a 1.5s emission, not a claim about how long the incident lasted. Every event carries an extent_source field of measured or fallback so the two are never confused downstream.
  • One incident, one tag — plus sub-tags. An accident reads as smoke, then as a gathered crowd; those were being emitted as three separate wrong events. Detections are now clustered by time regardless of class, and a single extra VLM call names the primary incident while everything else is retained as sub-tags. No causal lookup table: smoke is a symptom over a wrecked car and the incident itself over a thermal plant, so the judgement is asked of the model that can see which one it is looking at.
  • The sub-tags are kept, not discarded. The arena schema takes one class per event, which is right for scoring and wrong for whoever receives the alert. Sub-tags ship in incidents_detailed.json and on the incident timeline plot, and are folded into the submission's optional explanation field, which is scored as a bonus and never penalised.

Limitations & Next

The recall gap has a name: no memory between frames

Neither stage remembers anything across frames. A stalled vehicle looks identical to one waiting at a red light in any single frame — the only thing that makes it anomalous is that it's been true for fifteen seconds, which nothing here represents.

  • Confirmed by the data, not assumed: every fully-missed class (congestion, stalled vehicles, blocking, loitering) is duration-defined; every caught class is appearance-defined.
  • A cheap fix found by inspection: one video's obvious, dense traffic jam went undetected — likely because a normal-rule phrase ("vehicles waiting in an orderly queue at a red light") sits too close to congested traffic in embedding space. Testable in minutes by rewording the rule pool, no architecture change needed.
  • The real fix, not yet built: a short rolling frame buffer so duration-suspect candidates get sampled across 20–30 seconds instead of 2, letting the VLM directly compare "does this look the same as it did a while ago" — reusing the existing model call, no new tracker.
  • With more time: lightweight object tracking (centroid or IoU-matched) would give persistent per-object duration for stalled vehicles, loitering people and blocking vehicles through one mechanism instead of three heuristics — the more general answer, and the more expensive one to get right.
