# LAVAD — Harnessing LLMs for Training-free Video Anomaly Detection
arXiv 2404.01014 (CVPR 2024) · Univ. of Trento + FBK · https://lucazanella.github.io/lavad/

## Core contribution
First **fully training-free** VAD method. No training data, no fine-tuning, no normality
model — only frozen off-the-shelf models at inference. Motivation is explicitly ours:
"data collection is hard in video surveillance where **privacy issues hinder data
acquisition**", and trained VAD models fail to transfer across settings (day vs night).

## Exact method (5 frozen models, 3 components)
Frozen parts: captioner `C` (BLIP-2 ensemble), LLM (Llama-2-13b-chat), and ImageBind's
image / text / video encoders `E_I, E_T, E_V`.

**0. Caption every frame** (they sample every 16 frames for cost). Raw captions are noisy —
this is the failure mode they build the whole method around.

**1. Image-Text Caption Cleaning.** For frame `I_i`, don't use its own caption. Instead search
the captions of the **entire video** and take the one whose text embedding best matches this
frame's image embedding:
`Ĉ_i = argmax over all captions C of V of  <E_I(I_i), E_T(C)>`
Rationale: in a static-camera high-frame-rate video, a good caption for this scene almost
certainly exists *somewhere* in the video, possibly far away in time. Broken captions get
replaced by good ones from semantically similar frames. **Worth +3.8 AUC.**

**2. LLM-based Anomaly Scoring.** Cleaned captions still lack dynamics. Take a **T = 10 s**
window centred on frame `i`, sample N frames, feed the ordered caption list to the LLM with
prompt `P_S` = *"Please summarize what happened in few sentences, based on the following
temporal description of a scene. Do not include any unnecessary details or descriptions."* →
temporal summary `S_i`. Then ask the LLM to score `S_i` in [0,1] by picking one of **11
uniformly-spaced values** (classification, not regression — makes parsing reliable).
Context prompt `P_C` uses **impersonation**: *"If you were a law enforcement agency, how would
you rate the scene described on a scale from 0 to 1, with 0 representing a standard scene and
1 denoting a scene with suspicious activities?"* **Worth +7.58 AUC.**

**3. Video-Text Score Refinement.** Scores are text-only so far. Encode the video snippet
`V_i` with `E_V`, find the **K = 10** temporal summaries whose text embeddings are closest to
it, and take a softmax-weighted average of *their* anomaly scores. i.e. a frame's final score
is borrowed from visually-similar moments elsewhere in the video. **Worth +7.49 AUC.**
Threshold the refined scores to get anomalous intervals.

## 🔴 The single most important number in this paper, for us
**Zero-shot CLIP scores 53.16 AUC on UCF-Crime. Random is 50.00.**
ImageBind image 53.65, ImageBind video 55.78. Their explanation:
> *"VLMs are mostly trained to attend to foreground objects, rather than actions or the
> background information in an image that contributes to the judgment of anomalies."*

Meanwhile the same CLIP-style retrieval scored **0.269 mIoU and beat every VideoLLM** on
hour-scale *object/event grounding* (2606.12300). Same family of model, same kind of prompt:
**strong on "is the noun there", near-random on "is the behaviour anomalous."**

That is direct published evidence for our §4 thesis — that T\*-style single-frame,
noun-centric scoring will fail on behaviour queries — and it means our
**object-queries vs event-queries split in `eval/queries.json` is not a hunch, it's a
predicted result with a citation.** If our eval reproduces this gap on FlytBase footage,
that gap *is* the technical-depth story, whether or not our fix closes it.

## Stated / evident limitations
- **Cost.** An LLM summarisation call *and* an LLM scoring call per sampled frame (every 16
  frames). On an hour of 30 fps video that is ~6,750 sampled frames → ~13,500 LLM calls.
  No latency or throughput table is reported anywhere in the paper. **This is not real-time
  and they never claim it is.**
- Still 7.3 AUC below weakly-supervised SOTA. Training-free is not a free lunch.
- Caption cleaning assumes a **static camera at high frame rate** so that semantically
  redundant frames exist elsewhere in the video. **Explicitly stated.** Holds for CCTV,
  **breaks for moving drone footage** — every frame may be a novel viewpoint.
- Anomaly notion is baked into the prompt, not learned; no way to adapt to site-specific
  normality ("forklifts here are normal, at night they are not").

## What we steal for a 7-hour hackathon on CCTV/drone footage

1. **The caption-cleaning trick, repurposed as dedup + a free quality filter.** Nearest-caption
   retrieval over the whole video is ~5 lines given we already have SigLIP2 embeddings and
   captions in the index. On CCTV it should work *better* than on their data (more static
   redundancy). Bonus: caption→frame similarity is a cheap **caption-quality score** we can
   log — chunks whose caption does not match their own frames are hallucination candidates,
   which is a ready-made **failure case for the demo** (per our working agreements).

2. **Temporal summarisation over a sliding window is the cheapest event-awareness available.**
   Their +7.58 AUC came from summarising a 10 s caption window rather than scoring single
   frames. Our motion/event-aware scorer can literally start as: *concatenate the captions of
   N adjacent chunks + their motion-energy values, summarise, index the summary.* No new
   model, big conceptual gain over per-frame nouns, and it is one extra column in
   `enriched.parquet`.

3. **Anomaly score as a *retrievable field*, not a separate product.** Store a per-chunk
   `anomaly_score` at index time. Then "show me anything unusual near the loading bay" is a
   filter over the index, not a new pipeline. That is a genuinely FlytBase-shaped feature
   (their pitch is 70–85% false-alarm reduction) and it reuses the index we already built.

4. **Force the LLM to pick one of 11 discrete values.** Parsing-robust, and gives clean
   thresholding + a ROC curve for free. Adopt verbatim.

5. **Impersonation in the prompt, no anomaly prior.** +0.96 for free; and their result that
   the explicit "criminal activity" prior *hurts* is a useful warning — keep the context
   prompt about the *role* ("you are a site security operator"), not about what counts as an
   anomaly.

6. **AUC ROC as an extra metric.** We already need thresholds; frame-level AUC is
   threshold-agnostic and lets us plot a real ROC. Cheap addition to `evaluate.py`.

7. **Baseline discipline to copy exactly.** Their ZS CLIP / ZS ImageBind / LLaVA-direct rows
   are what make the paper convincing. Our equivalent three rows: **SigLIP2-only retrieval**,
   **caption-BM25-only**, **VLM-direct-on-uniform-frames**. Cheap to run, and they turn our
   results table from a single number into an argument.

## ⚠️ What NOT to do
Do **not** run LAVAD as specified (two LLM calls per sampled frame across hours of footage) —
it would consume the entire day's compute. Apply the LAVAD scoring loop **only to chunks that
survive the motion prefilter**, and treat the prefilter's reduction ratio as the headline
efficiency number. Also note the static-camera assumption breaks on drone footage: if the
11:00 dataset is mostly aerial, caption cleaning should be restricted within a scene/shot
rather than across the whole video.
